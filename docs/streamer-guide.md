# Your stream routine

StreamPulse works best when it supports the show you already want to make. Start with a simple routine, then add tools when they solve a real need.

If you have not connected StreamPulse to your chat and OBS yet, follow the [quick start](quickstart.md) first. For a tour of what is available, see [features for your stream](features.md).

## Before the stream: prepare the show

### 1. Open your workspace

Open StreamPulse, SocialStream Ninja, and OBS. Check the connection indicators on the StreamPulse dashboard and send a test chat message. If you use Twitch features, check that your Twitch account is connected too.

Look at the current session before you begin. If the dashboard offers **Start Session** and you are ready to collect this broadcast's activity, use it. If a session is already active, make sure it is the one you intend to use.

### 2. Decide what viewers will see

Use **Game Plan** to arrange tonight's games and keep future ideas separate from the current lineup. If you show a schedule overlay, check that its displayed games and dates match your plan.

Choose your starting, gameplay, break, and closing scenes in OBS. Use **Custom Overlay Studio** for a complete layout, or an overlay wizard to add a timer, goal, or other single element.

### 3. Prepare audience moments

If you plan to use the **Choice Randomizer**, review the choices before the reveal. Check the commands and recurring messages you want the **Chat Bot** to use, and pause anything that does not fit today's show.

Review alert rules and test the sounds you intend to play. Have the **Alert Queue** open where you can reach it; turn on **Hide unmatched** if you only want to review triggered alerts.

### 4. Rehearse the ending

In **Credits Studio**, check your sections, closing message, and special thanks. Preview the roll and listen to your outro audio in OBS.

Run tests while you are off-air, before the session you want to keep where practical. Simulated alerts and test activity can appear on your overlay or affect what you see in the current session.

### A short pre-stream check

- Your chat message reaches StreamPulse.
- The intended session is active.
- The right lineup and overlay are visible in each OBS scene.
- Overlay text is readable at your broadcast size.
- Alert and media sound levels are comfortable in an OBS recording.
- Your credits and closing scene are ready.

## During the stream: keep the focus on the audience

### Follow the plan, but leave room to change it

When you begin a game, use **Play Now** in Game Plan so your on-screen lineup reflects the current choice. Update the plan if the show changes. You can move to the next game without rebuilding the schedule overlay.

Use timers for starting screens or breaks so viewers know what to expect. Keep goals and other information visible only where they help the scene.

### Give interactions their own moment

Use the randomizer when you are ready for viewers to see the reveal. Check the result before moving on, especially when you are choosing the next game or challenge.

Let the Chat Bot handle routine links and replies, but adjust messages if they become repetitive or distract from the conversation.

### Manage alerts deliberately

Pause the alert queue during a sensitive or high-focus moment, then resume when there is room to acknowledge support. Replay a missed alert if appropriate; skip one if it should not be shown again.

Treat the queue as a production aid, not a requirement to interrupt every moment of the show.

### Keep your controls close and your notes off-screen

OBS docks can keep useful controls beside your scenes and audio mixer. If you use Bitfocus Companion and a Stream Deck, put frequently used actions on buttons you can find easily.

Keep Community Directory notes and other personal information in a window that is not captured by OBS. Use a game or window capture when appropriate, and check your scene before opening community records.

## After the stream: close, review, and reset

### 1. Thank your community before ending the session

Switch to your closing scene and run the credits while the current session's activity is still available. Add a personal thank-you where it matters.

Keep StreamPulse running until the outro is finished and you no longer need its overlays in OBS.

### 2. Finish the session intentionally

After the broadcast and credits, use **End Session** on the dashboard to archive the session and reset live activity for the next one. This does not stop your OBS broadcast or quit StreamPulse; end the broadcast in OBS separately.

Wait for confirmation. If StreamPulse reports a save or backup problem, address it before assuming the session is safely finished.

### 3. Take useful notes

Review session history and chat for moments you want to revisit. If you use **Clips (Beta)**, review suggested moments before deciding what to create or share.

Add brief community notes only where they will help future conversations. Be thoughtful about what you record and who can see it.

### 4. Set up the next show

Move finished games to the appropriate place in Game Plan, adjust the upcoming lineup, and note anything you want to change in your scenes or alerts.

Use **Backup & Restore** to keep a backup before major changes or updates. Store backups somewhere you control and avoid sharing them publicly, since they can include configuration, chat, and community information. Restore only while off-air.

When your overlays are no longer needed, quit StreamPulse from its application or tray menu. Closing the desktop window leaves it running in the background.

## Start small

A useful first routine is just **one overlay, a checked chat connection, and a closing credits roll**. Add Game Plan when you need a rundown, alerts when you want more control over celebrations, and the bot when repetitive chat tasks start taking your attention.
